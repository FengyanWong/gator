### 7.486 The pause timing test made deterministic: one lane for one cancelled candidate, both lanes held for two (2026-11-25, Claude; user: "go on", to the next step offered after record 7.485: the timing test that failed in 2 of the last 7 full runs)

**What was asked.** Record 7.485's session offered, as the next step it would take, the timing test that had failed in 2
of the last 7 full runs (once in this container, once on GitHub, where the workflow's second test step ran it again).
The user answered "go on".

**The failure.** `test_a_candidate_a_pause_stops_before_its_launch_is_cancelled_not_a_failure_of_the_link`
(`tests/workbench/test_wb_engine.py`) ran a mock run with two lanes, pressed Pause while one lane's third candidate was
between its authorization and its launch, and expected exactly one cancelled candidate. It failed by timing: records
7.196, 7.483 and 7.485 each saw it once, and run 4 of the workflow on GitHub needed its second test step for it.
Reproduced in this container: 32 runs, four at a time beside three busy loops, 3 failed, each with two cancelled
candidates where the test expects one.

**Why: the engine is right.** A run has one stop file (`control/stop-condition-jobs.json`). A pause writes it, and every
candidate handed to a lane and not yet launched sees it before its launch and is stopped: cancelled, "Candidate stopped
before nominal evaluation" (record 7.135). In each failing run both lanes had been handed their third candidate in the
same millisecond, as the scheduler does when both lanes finish together, and both were stopped and recorded correctly.
In a passing run the second lane's third candidate had not been handed out yet, or had passed its stop check and
launched before the stop file appeared: a reviewing agent found both lanes handed their candidates together in 18 of 60
loaded runs of the old test, and two cancellations in 5 of them. Only the test's count assumed a single candidate on its
way.

**The two tests.** The test keeps its name and now runs one lane, so exactly one candidate is on its way: one
cancellation, the run's last candidate. A new test, `test_a_pause_stops_the_candidate_on_its_way_in_every_lane`, runs
two lanes and holds each lane's third candidate after its authorization until the other lane's is held too; the second
to arrive presses Pause, and both wait for the stop file. Expected and found: exactly two cancelled candidates, one per
lane, each the last candidate of its lane, none an infrastructure failure. If the other lane never brings its third
candidate, the waiting lane gives up after 30 s and no Pause is pressed: the run completes, and the first assertion says
so. Only the tests changed.

**Proof.** Under the load of the reproduction, 32 runs of both tests gave 64 passes and no failure. Breakages of the
engine, each restored afterwards: record 7.135's fix undone (a stopped candidate recorded as `execution_error`) fails
both tests; candidates handed out after a pause, with both of the scheduler's guards removed (the loop's condition and
`_dispatch`'s own check), fails both, with 38 cancellations in the first and several per lane in the second. With only
the loop's condition removed, `_dispatch`'s own check kept the behaviour, and both passed. Two reviewing agents, the
second trying to refute the first, ran both tests about 450 and 80 more times in a dozen variants (other seeds, random
delays before an authorization, a slowed lane or scheduler, a reset as the third candidate), all passing, and found no
blocker. Their two corrections were taken: the new test's docstring said the old test failed whenever both lanes were
handed candidates together, where it failed only when the other lane had not yet reached its launch; and a lane whose
partner never came waited 30 s more for a stop file that could not appear. The full regression with four workers gave
2,921 passed and 46 skipped in 3 min 45 s, none failed.
