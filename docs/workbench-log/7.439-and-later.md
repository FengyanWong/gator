### 7.439 The model scan stops at its nesting limit for a subcircuit that places itself, instead of recursing until RecursionError (2026-10-29, Claude; user: "fix the bug")

**Found.** Named in 7.437 (found (8)): `model_scan.classify_model` follows subcircuits placed inside a subcircuit at
most four levels deep (`depth`), but the path search that decides passive families (`_paths`, called through
`_passive_path_evidence`) classified every element from depth 0 again. A subcircuit that places itself, directly or
through another, therefore recursed until Python's RecursionError, and reading a PDK whose model files hold one failed
instead of classifying its devices (`pdk_record` catches only its own errors).

**Changed.** `_paths` and `_passive_path_evidence` take the depth and pass it on (`src/gator_workbench/model_scan.py`),
so both routes stop at the same limit. Such a subcircuit is now classified 'other': an element past the limit is left
unclassified, as the active-device route already did. Subcircuits nested fewer than four levels deep are classified
exactly as before; only a deeper chain reached through passive paths now stops at the limit.

**Tests.** `tests/workbench/test_wb_model_paths.py`: a new synthetic test (a subcircuit placing itself, two placing each
other, an ordinary nested one) fails on the old code with RecursionError and passes now; the test's copy of the previous
traversal passes the depth on too. The model tests (paths, families, PDK): 79 passed. Full regression: 2,746 passed, 29
skipped; the ngspice gate: 34 passed. Evidence: `artifacts/local/reviews/fix-model-scan-recursion-2026-10-29/`.
